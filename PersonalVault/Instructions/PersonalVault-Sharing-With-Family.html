One project covers the whole family - each person still gets their own vault

Sharing Personal Vault with family

Different from sharing with a friend: here it's fine to hand out your own credentials.json — it only identifies the app to Google, not any person's data. Everyone still creates their own master secret, their own vault, and signs in with their own separate Google account for their own separate Drive file, even though you all share one Google Cloud project.

What to send / what to keep private

✓ Safe to share with family

  • The zipped publish\ folder (the app)
  • Your credentials.json, copied into that same folder

✕ Keep this private, always

  • Your vault.pvlt / data folder
  • Your master secret
1
Google Cloud Console → Google Auth Platform → Audience

Add each family member as a test user

You don't need to repeat the Google Cloud Console setup per person — in the same project you already created for your own Drive sync, go to Google Auth Platform → Audience → Test users → Add users, and add each family member's own Gmail address (up to 100). This is the one step that truly can't be automated: Google requires each person's own account to be explicitly allowed before they can sign in through your app's OAuth client.

2
Command prompt → PersonalVault project folder

Build a portable, self-contained copy

dotnet publish -c Release -r win-x64 --self-contained true -p:PublishSingleFile=true -p:IncludeNativeLibrariesForSelfExtract=true -o publish

Produces a publish\ folder with PersonalVault.exe and everything it needs bundled in (roughly 100-150 MB, since the .NET runtime is included — no separate install needed on their end).

3
File Explorer

Copy your credentials.json into that same folder

Put it right next to PersonalVault.exe inside publish\. This is safe to share — it only identifies the app to Google, it is not a credential to your data, and each family member still authorizes with their own Google account (added in step 1) and gets their own separate Drive file.

4
Zip & send

Zip the publish folder and send it

Email, USB drive, shared folder — however you'd share any file. Do not include your own vault.pvlt, data folder, or master secret in the zip.

5
Their PC

They unzip and run it — everything after this is automatic

  1. Unzip anywhere and double-click PersonalVault.exe — no install, no admin rights needed.
  2. The app automatically copies your bundled credentials.json into their own data folder the moment it sees one sitting next to the .exe — they never have to find that folder or copy anything by hand.
  3. They choose their own master secret: minimum 12 characters, with uppercase, lowercase, and a symbol. There's no password reset — losing it means losing the vault.
  4. The app then directly asks "Connect Google Drive now?" — clicking Yes pops the same one-time Google sign-in browser window you saw, just for their own account (the one you added in step 1).
✓ From there, their vault, their Drive file, and their secret are entirely their own — completely separate from yours and from every other family member's, even though you all got the app from the same zip and the same credentials.json.

Notes and troubleshooting

The things that actually come up sharing this within a family.
? Can I see their accounts, since we share a Google Cloud project?
No. The Google Cloud project only identifies the app to Google — each person's vault is its own AES-256-GCM encrypted file, on their own Drive, readable only with their own master secret. Nobody, including you, can see another family member's accounts.
? A family member wasn't prompted to sign in to Drive
This only happens automatically on a genuinely first run with credentials.json already in place. If they skipped it, they can do it anytime later from the tray menu → Sign in to Google Drive.
? "This app isn't verified" or a similar Google warning
Expected while the OAuth consent screen is in "Testing" mode (the normal state for a personal project) — this is exactly why step 1 (adding each person as a test user) matters. Click through the "Advanced" / "Go to (app name)" link Google shows for apps in testing.
? Someone hit the 100 test-user limit
That cap is per Google Cloud project, not per person. If you genuinely need more than 100 people on one project, Google's consent screen can be published beyond "Testing," which has its own separate verification requirements — unlikely to matter for a family, but worth knowing it's there.
? Want the one-time Share Account link feature too?
That's a separate, unrelated feature (unrelated to Drive backup) with its own fully self-service setup — see PersonalVault-Share-Setup-Guide.html in this same folder. Each person who wants to use it needs their own GitHub Pages site and their own Apps Script deployment; it can't be shared the way credentials.json can.
